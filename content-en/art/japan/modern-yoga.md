## Key points

- **Takahashi Yuichi's** (1828–1894) Salmon, painted between Meiji 8 and 12 (1875–79), is an Important Cultural Property held by **Tokyo University of the Arts**
- **Kuroda Seiki** (1866–1924) went to France in 1884, became a painter after studying under **Raphaël Collin** from 1886, returned in 1893 and founded the **Hakuba-kai** in 1896
- Kuroda's Lakeside (1897) was painted on the shore of Lake Ashi in Hakone with his wife Teruko as the model, and is an Important Cultural Property held by the Tokyo National Museum (Kuroda Memorial Hall)
- **Aoki Shigeru** (1882–1911) stayed at Mera beach in Tateyama, Chiba, from July 1904 and painted Bounty of the Sea there. Important Cultural Property, Artizon Museum (Ishibashi Foundation)
- **Kishida Ryūsei** (1891–1929) painted his daughter Reiko; Portrait of Reiko Smiling (1921) is an Important Cultural Property held by the Tokyo National Museum

## Period and background

The generation of the late Edo and early Meiji periods learned Western oil technique by trial and error. Kuroda, returning from study in France in the late 1880s–90s, brought the bright depiction of outdoor light, became the leading teacher of the Western-painting department of the Tokyo School of Fine Arts in 1896, and founded the Hakuba-kai. Aoki entered the school in 1900 and studied under Kuroda; in the Taishō era Ryūsei went his own way with minutely detailed realism. ==The four painters each took on a different stage: importing the technique (Yuichi), introducing plein-air painting (Kuroda), mythological subjects (Aoki) and detailed realism (Ryūsei).==

**Kuroda** went to France in February 1884 and in March entered the Institution Goffart in Paris. In 1886, on the advice of Yamamoto Hōsui, Fuji Masazō and Hayashi Tadamasa, he decided to become a painter and studied under Collin. His connection with law appears in the chronology as auditing classes at a law faculty in 1886 and entering a law school in 1887 (withdrawing in October). He returned in July 1893, became a lecturer at the Tokyo School of Fine Arts in May 1896 and founded the Hakuba-kai in June; he became Imperial Household Artist in 1910, a member of the House of Peers in 1920, and president of the Imperial Fine Arts Academy in July 1922. **Aoki**, eldest son of a former samurai of the Arima domain, entered the Western-painting department of the Tokyo School of Fine Arts in 1900 and learned Kuroda's plein-air method. In summer 1904 he stayed in Mera with Sakamoto Hanjirō, Fukuda Tane and others. The following year, 1905, a child (Fukuda Randō) was born to him and Fukuda Tane.

### Salmon

A hanging salmon painted in oil on paper. Dated to between Meiji 8 and 12, it is praised for showing the starting point of modern painting in its scientific eye for nature and lifelike depiction. ==One of the achievements of Western-style painting in the early Meiji years, it was designated an Important Cultural Property in 1967.== It belongs to Tokyo University of the Arts.

### Lakeside

According to the Tokyo National Research Institute for Cultural Properties, it was painted in summer 1897 while Kuroda and Teruko were staying in Hakone to escape the heat. Teruko recalled that he asked her to sit on a stone by the lake, and that with rainy and foggy days in between it took about a month. It renders the muted landscape and damp air of a Japanese highland summer in pale tones and smooth brushwork. It was shown at the second Hakuba-kai exhibition in 1897 under the title "Escaping the Heat" and at the 1900 Paris Exposition, where only Wisdom, Impression, Sentiment won a prize. ==The model, Kaneko Tane (later Teruko), formally married Kuroda in 1922.== In 1967 it was used as the design of a 15-yen stamp.

### Bounty of the Sea

According to the Artizon Museum, in mid-July 1904 Aoki, just graduated from the Tokyo School of Fine Arts, went on a sketching trip to Mera beach in Tateyama, Chiba, with Sakamoto Hanjirō, Morita Tsunetomo, Fukuda Tane and others, and painted it during a stay of about a month and a half. Ten naked men carry three sharks, marching in two columns along the beach. Aoki himself did not say what prompted the work; scholars point to the frieze of the Parthenon and the Pre-Raphaelites.

### Portrait of Reiko Smiling

According to e-Kokuhō, it dates from Taishō 10 (1921), oil on canvas, 44.2 × 36.4 cm, Tokyo National Museum (A-10568). It shows his beloved daughter Reiko at the age of eight; her smile with raised corners of the mouth is said to recall the Mona Lisa, and the shawl painted down to the texture of its wool is a feature. Ryūsei began painting Reiko around 1918; there are said to be about 30 oil paintings alone and more than 100 including watercolours (some sources say about 70). It was designated an Important Cultural Property on 22 June 1971.

## How to look

### Whom they learned from

Kuroda studied under Collin, Aoki under Kuroda. Differences in teachers appear on the canvas as differences in the texture of things (the flesh of the salmon), the colour of outdoor light (the pale tones of Lakeside), and the sources of composition (the Parthenon frieze).

### What they painted

The subjects change: a fish (still life), a wife (figure and landscape), mythical fishermen (a group), a daughter (portrait). Only Bounty of the Sea shows a scene that never existed; the other three show things close at hand.

### Designation and ownership

Three of the four are Important Cultural Properties, and their owners are divided between a university (Tokyo University of the Arts), a national museum (Tokyo National Museum) and a foundation (Ishibashi Foundation). Salmon was designated in 1967.

## FAQ

### Who was the model for Lakeside?

Kuroda's wife Teruko. It was painted in summer 1897 while they were staying in Hakone to escape the heat.

### What did Kuroda go to Paris to study?

According to the chronology he went to study in February 1884; his connection with law appears as auditing classes at a law faculty in 1886 and entering a law school in 1887. In 1886 he began studying under Collin and turned to painting.

### Where was Bounty of the Sea painted?

At Mera beach in Tateyama, Chiba, during a stay of about a month and a half from mid-July 1904.

### When was Salmon painted?

Between Meiji 8 and 12 (1875–79). It belongs to Tokyo University of the Arts and was designated an Important Cultural Property in 1967.

## Key points

- **Sesshū** (1420–c. 1506) was born in Akahama, Bitchū Province (Sōja, Okayama). As a boy he entered **Shōkoku-ji** in Kyoto, studying Zen under Shunrin Shūtō and painting under Shūbun
- Before 1464 he moved to Suō Province under the patronage of the Ōuchi family; **in 1467 (Ōnin 1) he sailed to Ming China on an official mission ship**, returning in 1469
- His National Treasures include Autumn and Winter Landscapes, View of Amanohashidate, Huike Offering His Arm, Landscape of the Four Seasons (handscroll), Splashed-ink Landscape and Landscape
- Autumn and Winter Landscapes is at the **Tokyo National Museum** (bought from Manshu-in in 1936), View of Amanohashidate at the **Kyoto National Museum**, and Huike Offering His Arm at Sainen-ji in Aichi
- View of Amanohashidate is painted on 21 small sheets of different sizes pasted together and shows corrections, so it is thought to be a draft

## Period and background

Ink painting in the Muromachi period was carried by painter-monks who studied Chinese Song and Yuan painting in the Five Mountain Zen temples of Kyoto. Sesshū studied under Shūbun at Shōkoku-ji and in 1467, as the Ōnin War began, sailed to Ming China on a mission ship, seeing Chinese pictures of Zen patriarchs and other works at first hand. After returning he set up the Unkoku-an studio in Yamaguchi in Suō and worked under the Ōuchi. ==His late work used the compositional methods of landscape painting he had seen in China to reconstruct real Japanese scenery.==

### National Treasures

Works by Sesshū designated National Treasures include Autumn and Winter Landscapes, View of Amanohashidate, Huike Offering His Arm, Landscape of the Four Seasons, Splashed-ink Landscape and Landscape. The three discussed here are divided between Tokyo, Kyoto and Aichi: Autumn and Winter Landscapes and View of Amanohashidate belong to the national museums, Huike Offering His Arm to a temple.

### Autumn and Winter Landscapes

According to e-Kokuhō, it dates from the Muromachi period, late 15th – early 16th century: ink on paper, two scrolls, each 47.7 × 30.2 cm, Tokyo National Museum (A-1398). The museum bought it from Manshu-in in Kyoto in 1936. The left scroll is a snow scene showing winter; scenery is layered around the vertical line of a boldly cropped cliff, and the degree of abstraction is high.

### View of Amanohashidate

According to the Kyoto National Museum, it has no signature or seal, and is regarded as Sesshū's from the brushwork, composition and handwriting of the place names. At the centre are the white sand and green pines of Amanohashidate and the pagoda of Chion-ji; above, across the Aso Sea, the town of Fuchū and the buildings and mountain of Nariai-ji; below, Miyazu Bay and the Kunda peninsula. It is based on the real view, but Nariai-ji's mountain is extremely high, the town of Fuchū is stretched sideways, and the scene is drawn from a high viewpoint that does not exist. ==It is painted on paper made of 21 small sheets of different sizes pasted together irregularly and shows corrections, so it was most likely a draft rather than a finished painting.== Taking the rebuilding of the Chion-ji pagoda in 1501 as a reference, the leading view dates it to Sesshū's last years, in his eighties.

### Huike Offering His Arm

According to the Kyoto National Museum, it was painted in Meiō 5 (1496), 199.9 cm high and 113.6 cm wide, in ink and light colour on paper. The inscription reads "Painted respectfully by Sesshū, First Seat of Tiantong in Siming, aged 77". It shows the moment when Shenguang (later Huike) asked to become a pupil of Bodhidharma, the first patriarch of Zen, who was meditating at Shaolin Temple on Mount Song; refused, he cut off his own left forearm one winter day to show his resolve. Huike later became the second patriarch of Zen. Sesshū appears to have drawn on Chinese pictures of Zen patriarchs he saw in China, and Ming paintings with similar compositions survive. No other example shows the severed arm itself; the level ground, the similar outlines of the two facing figures, and the geometric arrangement of rock texture strokes enclosing about three quarters of the picture are its features. It was dedicated to Sainen-ji in Tokoname, Owari, in 1532 by the local warrior Saji Tamesada.

## How to look

### Shades of ink

Ink painting shows depth and season with nothing but the shades of ink and the speed of the brush. The winter scene of Autumn and Winter Landscapes uses a vertical cliff in dark ink and the white of unpainted paper for snow.

### Real view and composition

View of Amanohashidate rebuilds the real view of Miyazu into the bird's-eye composition learned from Chinese painting. The exaggerated mountain of Nariai-ji and the stretched town of Fuchū are the result of composition, not sketching.

### Inscriptions and provenance

Huike Offering His Arm carries an inscription with the year and the painter's age; View of Amanohashidate has no signature or seal. For the first, the record of its dedication in 1532, and for the second, the year the pagoda was rebuilt, are the keys to dating. Autumn and Winter Landscapes was handed down at Manshu-in in Kyoto and bought by the Tokyo National Museum in 1936. Tracing which temple each work was kept in and when it entered a museum shows how Sesshū's works were preserved within Zen temples.

## FAQ

### When did Sesshū go to China?

He sailed on a mission ship in 1467 (Ōnin 1) and returned in 1469. If he was born in 1420, he was 48 by Japanese count.

### Which Kyoto temple did he enter as a boy?

Shōkoku-ji. He moved there as a child from Hōfuku-ji in Bitchū, studying Zen under Shunrin Shūtō and painting under Shūbun.

### Why is View of Amanohashidate thought to be a draft?

Because it is painted on paper made of 21 small sheets of different sizes pasted together irregularly, and shows corrections. It has been suggested that a separate finished painting existed.

### Who is shown in Huike Offering His Arm?

Bodhidharma, the first patriarch of Zen, and Shenguang (later Huike, the second patriarch), who cut off his left forearm to ask to become his pupil. It is inscribed with the year 1496 and the painter's age, 77.

## Key points

- Born on 8 February 1716, eldest son of a greengrocer wholesaler in Kyoto. The Nihon Daihyakka Zensho names the shop as Masugen at Takakura Nishiki-kōji
- At 40 he handed the family business to his younger brother and, never marrying, devoted himself to painting
- From 1757 to 1766 he painted the 30 scrolls of the Colorful Realm of Living Beings and gave them to Shōkoku-ji in Kyoto

Jakuchū was a painter who especially loved to paint roosters. The Sannomaru Shōzōkan describes the fine detail and bold compositions of the Colorful Realm of Living Beings as features not found in other painters of his time. ==His lay name "Jakuchū" is said to have been given by the monk Daiten of Shōkoku-ji.==

## Style

- Fine detail and bold compositions
- Both bird-and-flower paintings in colour and ink paintings

The Colorful Realm of Living Beings is a set of bird-and-flower paintings in colour on silk, with roosters at the centre among many animals and plants. The Sekai Daihyakka Jiten names the wall paintings of the Rokuon-ji Ōshoin as his masterpiece in ink.

Jakuchū gave the Colorful Realm of Living Beings to Shōkoku-ji together with the Shaka Triad, and they were hung at memorial services. In 1889 (Meiji 22) Shōkoku-ji presented them to the Imperial Household.

## Where to see his work

The Museum of the Imperial Collections, Sannomaru Shōzōkan (East Gardens of the Imperial Palace, Tokyo) holds the Colorful Realm of Living Beings. Some scrolls are shown in each exhibition; check the museum's website.

## FAQ

### What was the family business?

A greengrocer wholesaler on Nishiki-kōji in Kyoto. Jakuchū was the eldest son.

### What is his masterpiece?

The 30 scrolls of the Colorful Realm of Living Beings, a National Treasure held by the Museum of the Imperial Collections, Sannomaru Shōzōkan.

### When did he die?

On 10 September 1800 (Kansei 12), in Kyoto.

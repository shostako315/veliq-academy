## Key points

- Usually said to have been born around 1753, though accounts differ. He studied under Toriyama Sekien
- Discovered by the publisher Tsutaya Jūzaburō, he began issuing bust portraits of beauties around 1790
- In 1804 (Bunka 1) he was censured and punished by the shogunate; he died in 1806

Utamaro was the ukiyo-e artist who established his own style with ōkubi-e, bust portraits showing women large from the chest up. The Sekai Daihyakka Jiten gives the series Ten Classes of Women's Physiognomy and Ten Studies in Female Physiognomy as examples. ==After Tsutaya Jūzaburō was punished in the shogunate's crackdown of 1791, Utamaro restored his fortunes with series of bust portraits.==

## Style

- Bust portraits (ōkubi-e) showing women large from the chest up
- After the kyōka picture books Ehon mushi erami, Shiohi no tsuto and Momo chidori, he moved to bust portraits

The Nihon Daihyakka Zensho says Utamaro devised the bust portrait of beauties in the early 1790s and established his own style.

## Where to see his work

The Tokyo National Museum holds Young Woman Blowing a Poppin. Ukiyo-e prints are sensitive to light and are shown only for short periods; check the museum's website.

## FAQ

### Who taught him?

Toriyama Sekien.

### Which publisher did he work with?

Tsutaya Jūzaburō, who discovered him around 1784.

### Why was he punished?

In 1804 prints based on the Taikōki were censured by the shogunate. He was sentenced to prison and manacles.

## Key points

- Born in Kagoshima on 29 June 1866; went to France in 1884 to study law
- He studied under the painter Raphaël Collin and, returning in 1893, brought a new bright-coloured style to Japan
- In 1896 he founded the Hakuba-kai and taught in the new Western-painting department of the Tokyo School of Fine Arts. He died on 15 July 1924

Kuroda Seiki was one of the painters who shaped modern Western-style painting in Japan. Cultural Heritage Online notes that for his bright colours he was called the centre of the "new school" in contrast to earlier Western-style painters. ==The Institute of Art Research founded under his will led to today's Tokyo National Research Institute for Cultural Properties.==

## Style

- Outdoor light and pale colours
- Japanese subjects painted with Western technique

Lakeside paints a Japanese subject (a woman in a yukata cooling herself with a fan) with Western technique, using outdoor light and pale bluish colours while holding back the gloss of oil paint. Cultural Heritage Online calls it a representative example of Japanese oil painting.

Reading was painted in Grez-sur-Loing with Maria Billault, the daughter of his landlord, as the model. The model for Lakeside was Kaneko Taneko (Kuroda Teruko), who later became his wife.

## Where to see his work

Kuroda's works are held by the Tokyo National Museum and shown at certain times at the Kuroda Memorial Hall in Ueno. Check the websites of the Tokyo National Museum and the Kuroda Memorial Hall for viewing days.

## FAQ

### Where was he born?

In Kagoshima, on 29 June 1866.

### Why did he go to France?

To study law, in 1884. He later decided to become a painter.

### Who taught him?

The French painter Raphaël Collin.

### What was the Hakuba-kai?

A society of Western-style painters founded in 1896 by Kuroda, Kume Keiichirō and others. Lakeside was shown at its second exhibition in 1897 under the title "Escaping the Heat".

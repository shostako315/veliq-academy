## Key points

- Born on 1 May 1733, the second son of a farming family in Anao village, Tanba Province (now Kameoka, Kyoto Prefecture)
- He studied under Ishida Yūtei in Kyoto and, painting megane-e, encountered Western linear perspective and shading
- He created a style based on sketching from life and, with his pupils, formed the Maruyama school. He died on 17 July 1795, aged 63

Maruyama Ōkyo came to Kyoto in his mid-teens to study painting. Not content with decorative styles, he moved towards realism. ==His National Treasure Pine Trees in Snow at the Mitsui Memorial Museum is painted in ink, gold paint and the white of the paper alone.==

## Style

- He joined sketching from life with traditional decorative painting
- The mokkotsu technique, painting without outlines

The Mitsui Memorial Museum says that in Pine Trees in Snow Ōkyo painted the pines without outlines in the mokkotsu technique, and that the work shows well his fresh style uniting sketching from life with traditional decorative painting.

## Where to see his work

The Mitsui Memorial Museum (Nihonbashi, Tokyo) holds Pine Trees in Snow. It is shown only at certain times; check the museum's website.

## FAQ

### Where was he born?

In Anao village, Tanba Province (now Kameoka, Kyoto Prefecture), on 1 May 1733.

### Who taught him?

Ishida Yūtei of the Tsurusawa school, in Kyoto.

### Who were his pupils?

His son Ōzui, Nagasawa Rosetsu, Matsumura Gekkei (Goshun), Yoshimura Kōkei, Komai Genki, Yamaguchi Soken and others. They are called the Maruyama school.

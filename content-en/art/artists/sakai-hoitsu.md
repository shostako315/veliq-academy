## Key points

- Born in Edo in 1761, the younger brother of Sakai Tadazane, lord of the Himeji domain
- In 1797, at 37, he took Buddhist vows; he later named his home in Negishi the Uge-an
- In 1815 he held the hundredth-anniversary memorial for Ogata Kōrin and published One Hundred Paintings by Kōrin, rooting Rinpa in Edo

Born the younger brother of a daimyo, Hōitsu passed through haikai poetry, kyōka, ukiyo-e and the Kanō school before devoting himself to Ogata Kōrin, a Kyoto painter of a century earlier. ==He carried on Kōrin's style in Edo.== The line was continued by his pupils such as Suzuki Kiitsu, and is called Edo Rinpa.

## Life

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1761 (Hōreki 11, 7th month, 1st day) | Born in Edo; his elder brother is Sakai Tadazane, lord of Himeji |
| Youth | Studies the Kanō school, then Utagawa Toyoharu's ukiyo-e and Sō Shiseki's sketching from life in the manner of Shen Nanpin; studies haikai under Baba Zongi |

| 1823 | Publishes Kenzan's Surviving Works, a collection of works by Ogata Kenzan |
| 4 Jan 1829 (Bunsei 11, 11th month, 29th day) | Dies in Edo |

He gave illness as his reason for taking vows. In his last years he lived at the Uge-an in Negishi. ==Though born into a daimyo family, by taking vows he gained a position free of domain duties to live for painting and letters.== His work of copying the works of Kōrin and his brother Kenzan and gathering them into woodblock books also spread the works themselves.

## Style

- He kept Kōrin's compositions and gold and silver grounds while painting plants in fine detail
- He shows rain and wind not with raindrops or wind lines but through the attitude of the plants
- Through the "back" and "copies" of Kōrin's works, he held a dialogue with Kōrin

His masterpiece, [Flowering Plants of Summer and Autumn](/en/art/artworks/hoitsu-natsuaki-kusa/), was painted on the back of Kōrin's Wind God and Thunder God. In the summer grasses on the right, lilies and bindweed droop their leaves towards the ground while rainwater runs from the earth; without painting raindrops, it shows a violent summer evening shower. In the autumn grasses on the left, kudzu, thoroughwort, pampas grass and wild grape are blown hard by the wind, and the floating wild grape and overturned leaves show its strength.

The summer grasses are behind the thunder god, the autumn grasses behind the wind god. ==Summer grasses beaten by the rain the thunder god brings, autumn grasses blown by the wind the wind god raises — front and back form a single story.== Against the gold of the front, the back has a silver ground, a contrast of heaven and earth.

Flowering Plants of Summer and Autumn was commissioned by Tokugawa Harusada, head of the Hitotsubashi family and father of the eleventh shogun Tokugawa Ienari. ==For conservation the front and back were separated in 1974 (Shōwa 49), and they are now separate screens.==

Flowers and Plants of the Four Seasons along a Stream joins two gold two-panel screens with a stream of ultramarine and lines up the plants of the four seasons from right to left. Its bright pigments and absence of shading follow Rinpa practice, while plants placed behind overlapping grasses create depth. Each screen is 162.0 × 172.0 cm.

Birds and Flowers of the Four Seasons is a pair of handscrolls dated 1818 (Bunka 15); the first scroll alone is 712.5 cm long. Daily Kannon is one of the images of Kannon that Hōitsu painted as a daily devotion after taking vows; its seal reads "one of 33 scrolls of the daily practice, fifth month of Bunsei 7", and it is signed "Tōkaku-in Hōitsu".

## Where to see his work

The Tokyo National Museum holds both Kōrin's Wind God and Thunder God and Hōitsu's Flowering Plants of Summer and Autumn, separated from its back. The museum also holds Flowers and Plants of the Four Seasons along a Stream, Birds and Flowers of the Four Seasons, Daily Kannon, Hanshan, Paintings on Fans and others, making it one of the museums with the most works by Hōitsu. Check the museum's schedule before going. ==Because front and back remain in the same museum, with good timing both can be compared.== Birds and Flowers of the Twelve Months belongs to the Imperial Household Agency.

## FAQ

### Was Hōitsu a pupil of Kōrin?

Not directly. Kōrin died in 1716; Hōitsu, born in 1761, studied Kōrin's works a century later and held his hundredth-anniversary memorial.

### Why was Flowering Plants of Summer and Autumn on the back of Kōrin's screens?

==Because Tokugawa Harusada of the Hitotsubashi family commissioned it to be painted on the back of Kōrin's Wind God and Thunder God.== They were separated in 1974 for conservation.

### What is Edo Rinpa?

The line of Rinpa painting carried on in Edo by Hōitsu and his pupils such as Suzuki Kiitsu.

### Who were his pupils?

Suzuki Kiitsu, Ikeda Koson and others.

### What were his real name and art names?

His real name was Sakai Tadanao, his common name Eihachi. He used many art names, including Toryō, Ōson, Uge-an and Teihakushi.

### Did he also write haikai and kyōka?

He studied haikai under Baba Zongi, with the haikai name Toryō, and used the name Shiriyake no Sarundo for kyōka.

### When did he take vows?

In 1797 (Kansei 9), at 37, citing illness, under Monnyo of Nishi Honganji.

### What is One Hundred Paintings by Kōrin?

A book of reduced copies of Kōrin's works published for the 1815 centenary of Kōrin's death. A second part followed later.

### What does Flowering Plants of Summer and Autumn show?

On the right, summer grasses — lilies and bindweed drooping in the rain; on the left, autumn grasses — kudzu, thoroughwort, pampas grass and wild grape blown by the wind. They are painted on a silver ground.

### How do Kōrin's Wind God and Thunder God and Hōitsu's screens pair?

Kōrin's front shows the heavenly wind and thunder gods on gold; Hōitsu's back shows earthly plants on silver. Behind the thunder god come summer grasses beaten by rain, behind the wind god autumn grasses blown by wind.

### Who was the patron Tokugawa Harusada?

Head of the Hitotsubashi family and father of the eleventh shogun Tokugawa Ienari.

### What is Kenzan's Surviving Works?

A woodblock book published in 1823 copying the works of Kōrin's younger brother Ogata Kenzan. Hōitsu copied and spread the works of both Kōrin and Kenzan.

### When was Flowering Plants of Summer and Autumn separated from Kōrin's screens?

In 1974 (Shōwa 49). For conservation the front and back were separated; they are now separate screens in the Tokyo National Museum.

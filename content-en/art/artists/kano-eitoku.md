## Key points

- Born in Kyoto on 13 January 1543. His father was Kanō Shōei, his grandfather Kanō Motonobu
- He painted the wall paintings of Oda Nobunaga's Azuchi Castle and Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Osaka Castle and Jurakudai palace
- He died on 14 September 1590, aged 48. Cypress Trees, from the same year, is regarded as one of his last works

Kanō Eitoku's given names were Genshirō and later Kuninobu; Eitoku is his art name. With screens and sliding doors showing great trees and beasts on gold, he decorated the castles and palaces of the Momoyama period. ==The right screen of Chinese Lions at the Sannomaru Shōzōkan is accepted as his work on the strength of a certificate by Kanō Tan'yū, and is one of the few works securely attributed to him.==

| 1590 (Tenshō 18) | Cypress Trees, said to be sliding doors for the residence of Prince Hachijō |
| 14 Sep 1590 | Dies aged 48 |

## Style

- He painted thick trunks and powerful beasts on a large scale against gold
- He made many wall paintings for the large rooms of castles and palaces

Cultural Heritage Online says that the bold forms and deep colours of the twisting trunk and spreading branches in Cypress Trees express the taste of the age. In the right screen of Chinese Lions, a male and female lion walk among rocks, drawn with a powerful brush.

## Where to see his work

Cypress Trees is held by the Tokyo National Museum (Ueno), Chinese Lions by the Museum of the Imperial Collections, Sannomaru Shōzōkan (East Gardens of the Imperial Palace). Both are shown only for limited periods; check each museum's website.

## FAQ

### Whose son was he?

The son of Kanō Shōei and grandson of Kanō Motonobu.

### Which castles did he decorate?

Oda Nobunaga's Azuchi Castle, and Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Osaka Castle and Jurakudai.

### When did he die?

On 14 September 1590 (Tenshō 18), aged 48.

### Which of his works can be seen in Tokyo?

Cypress Trees at the Tokyo National Museum and Chinese Lions at the Museum of the Imperial Collections, Sannomaru Shōzōkan.

## Key points

- An oil painting by van Gogh from 1889. Held by the National Museum of Western Art, Tokyo
- Long regarded as a work from the end of the Arles period; at the 1985 van Gogh exhibition it was proposed to show a corner of the asylum garden at Saint-Rémy
- The technique of laying "undulating" strokes side by side, prominent later at Saint-Rémy, can already be seen

In December 1888, after his conflict with Gauguin, van Gogh suffered his first attack. Six months later, on 8 May 1889, he moved to the asylum at Saint-Rémy. The doctors confined his movements and work to the asylum garden, so in May he seems to have painted mainly views of the garden. ==In a letter he wrote that an overgrown garden and a large stand of pines were subject enough.==

## What it shows

Roses, as the title says. The museum cites the view that it shows a corner of the asylum garden.

## How it was made

According to the museum, after parting from Gauguin, van Gogh moved away from the flat style influenced by Gauguin and back to his own rough brushwork. The technique of laying "undulating" strokes side by side, prominent in the Saint-Rémy period, already appears here.

## What to look at in front of it

- The areas of "undulating" strokes laid side by side, a technique that leads to the later Saint-Rémy works
- Traces of the return from Gauguin's flat style to rough brushwork
- The small canvas, 33 × 41.3 cm

## Where to see it

National Museum of Western Art (Ueno, Tokyo). Works on view in the permanent collection change with rotations and loans. Check the museum's website.

## FAQ

### Where was it painted?

The view that it shows the garden of the asylum at Saint-Rémy was put forward at the 1985 van Gogh exhibition. Before that it was regarded as a work from the end of the Arles period.

### When was it painted?

In 1889. Van Gogh moved to the asylum on 8 May that year.

### How large is it?

33 cm high and 41.3 cm wide.

## Key points

- Monet's Water Lilies of 1916, about two metres square. Held by the National Museum of Western Art, Tokyo
- The whole picture is covered by the surface of the pond
- Signed and dated "Claude Monet 1916" at lower left

Monet lived in Giverny near Paris from 1883, and in 1893 bought the land in front of his house to make a Japanese-style garden. He floated water lilies on the pond, built an arched bridge and planted willows and shrubs on the banks. Shut away in this world of water, he painted the Water Lilies series. ==The National Museum of Western Art regards this canvas as one of the finest of the late series.==

## What it shows

The surface of the pond, the water lilies floating on it, and the colours of what is reflected in the water. The whole picture is covered by the water, so that the viewer feels as if standing in the pond.

## How it was made

Monet built a large glass-walled studio in a corner of the garden and, moving an easel on wheels, painted the pond as it changed with the hours, the reflections on the water and the colours of the flowers. Early in the series he also painted willows, the arched bridge and evening skies, but gradually narrowed his attention to the surface of the pond alone. The museum explains that the brushwork and colour of the flowers and water here differ greatly from early Impressionist technique and take on a severity that could almost be called Expressionist.

## What to look at in front of it

- The water surface covering the whole picture
- The brushwork for the flowers and water, and how it differs from early Impressionist works
- The signature "Claude Monet 1916" at lower left

## Where to see it

National Museum of Western Art (Ueno, Tokyo). Works on view in the permanent collection change with rotations and loans. Check the museum's website.

## FAQ

### When was it painted?

In 1916; it is inscribed "Claude Monet 1916" at lower left.

### What place does it show?

The pond of the Japanese-style garden Monet made at his home in Giverny.

### How large is it?

200.5 cm high and 201 cm wide.

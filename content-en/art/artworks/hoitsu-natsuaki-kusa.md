## Key points

- Screens on silver that Sakai Hōitsu later painted on the back of Ogata Kōrin's Wind God and Thunder God
- Behind the thunder god he painted summer grasses beaten by rain; behind the wind god, autumn grasses bending in the wind
- In 1974 (Shōwa 49) the front and back were separated for conservation and are now separate screens

Hōitsu painted the screens on commission from Hitotsubashi Harusada, head of the Hitotsubashi family and father of the eleventh shogun Tokugawa Ienari. Where Kōrin's front shows heavenly gods on gold, Hōitsu's back shows earthly plants on silver. ==It is built as a pairing of gold and silver, heaven and earth.==

## What it shows

The right screen shows summer grasses. The leaves of lilies, bindweed and other plants droop towards the ground, where rainwater pools and flows. Not a single raindrop is painted, yet the scene after a violent evening shower is clear.

The left screen shows autumn grasses. Kudzu, thoroughwort, pampas grass, wild grape and others bend in a wind blowing from right to left. Wild-grape vines flying in the air and the colour of leaves turned over by the wind show its strength.

## How it was made

Colour on paper laid with silver leaf. The silver recalls moonlight, and the plants of summer and autumn are painted in fresh colours. The summer grasses were originally behind the thunder god and are wet with the rain he brings; the autumn grasses were behind the wind god and are blown by the wind he raises. Cultural Heritage Online says that Hōitsu, who revered Kōrin, answered Kōrin's picture in several ways.

## What to look at in front of it

- Right screen: the pools and streams of water on the ground; the rain itself is not painted
- Left screen: the colour of the overturned leaves and the wild-grape vines floating in the air
- The silver ground, paired with the gold of Kōrin's front

## Where to see it

Tokyo National Museum (Ueno, Tokyo). The museum also owns Kōrin's Wind God and Thunder God, which was the front. It is shown only at certain times. Check the museum's website for the schedule.

## FAQ

### Why was it painted on the back of Kōrin's screens?

On commission from Hitotsubashi Harusada. It was painted on the back of Kōrin's Wind God and Thunder God; the two sides were separated in 1974 for conservation.

### Who commissioned it?

Hitotsubashi Harusada, head of the Hitotsubashi family and father of the eleventh shogun Tokugawa Ienari.

### Is the ground gold or silver?

Silver. Kōrin's picture on the front has a gold ground.

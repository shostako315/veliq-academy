## Key points

- A print of a beauty showing a young woman from the chest up, large, blowing a glass toy called a poppin
- One sheet from Ten Classes of Women's Physiognomy, Utamaro's series of bust portraits of beauties
- Held by the Tokyo National Museum. Ōban size, 38.1 × 24.5 cm

Young Woman Blowing a Poppin is one sheet from Kitagawa Utamaro's series Ten Classes of Women's Physiognomy. The Sekai Daihyakka Jiten cites this series as an example of how Utamaro established his own style of bust portraits (ōkubi-e) of beauties in the early 1790s. ==The face and the hand holding the poppin take up most of the picture.==

## What it shows

A young woman holding a glass toy, a poppin, to her lips and blowing it. Only the figure from the chest up fills the sheet.

## How it was made

A colour woodblock print (nishiki-e) in ōban size. Utamaro studied under Toriyama Sekien and was discovered by the publisher Tsutaya Jūzaburō. In 1788 he published the kyōka picture book Ehon mushi erami, and from around 1790 began issuing bust portraits of beauties. In 1804 (Bunka 1) prints based on the Taikōki were censured by the shogunate and he was sentenced to prison and manacles; he died in 1806.

## What to look at in front of it

- The fingers holding the poppin and the glass tube at her lips
- How much of the sheet is given to the face and hand
- The composition cropped at the chest

## Where to see it

Tokyo National Museum (Ueno, Tokyo). Ukiyo-e prints are sensitive to light and are shown only for short periods. Check the museum's website for the schedule.

## FAQ

### What is Ten Classes of Women's Physiognomy?

One of Utamaro's series of bust portraits of beauties. Cultural Heritage Online also lists Woman Reading a Letter and Woman Blowing Tobacco Smoke from the same series.

### When was it made?

The museum records it as Edo period, 18th century.

## Key points

- A handscroll about 40 m long in ink on silk, regarded as the masterpiece of Yokoyama Taikan's life
- A drop of water on a mountain flows from a stream to a great river and the sea; a wave becomes a dragon rising to the sky, and returns to a drop
- Shown at the tenth Inten exhibition in 1923 (Taishō 12). Important Cultural Property

Metempsychosis is an ink painting that follows the changes of a single drop of water from one end of a scroll to the other. The National Museum of Modern Art, Tokyo explains that through the flow of water it expresses how all things are constantly changing. ==It is 55.3 cm high and 4,070 cm (40.7 m) long.==

## What it shows

Water vapour in the air becomes a mountain stream, a great river, and flows out to sea. A wave of the sea becomes a dragon rising into the sky, and the dragon returns to a drop of water to begin a new life.

## How it was made

Ink alone on silk (kenpon bokuga); Taikan uses many different ink techniques. Cultural Heritage Online says that Taikan's effort to create a new style of ink painting bore fruit in this vast composition.

Taikan was born in Mito in 1868. In 1889 he entered the Tokyo School of Fine Arts in its first class, and in 1898 joined Okakura Tenshin and others in founding the Japan Art Institute. In 1914 he revived the Institute; its exhibition is the Inten.

## What to look at in front of it

- The mountain-stream scenes and the scene where the sea's wave becomes a dragon, distinguished in ink alone
- The contrast between dark and pale ink
- The length of the scroll unrolled in the display case

## Where to see it

The National Museum of Modern Art, Tokyo (Takebashi, Tokyo). The whole scroll is unrolled only at certain times. Check the museum's website for the schedule.

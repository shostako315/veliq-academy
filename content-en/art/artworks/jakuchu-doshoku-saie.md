## Key points

- A series of 30 hanging scrolls that Itō Jakuchū painted over about ten years, with roosters at the centre among many animals and plants
- Jakuchū himself gave them to Shōkoku-ji temple in Kyoto, where they were hung at memorial services together with his Shaka Triad
- Shōkoku-ji presented them to the Imperial Household in 1889 (Meiji 22); they were designated a National Treasure in 2021

Colorful Realm of Living Beings is a set of 30 bird-and-flower paintings in colour on silk. The dates on the scrolls range from 1757–58 (Hōreki 7–8) to 1766 (Meiwa 3). The Sannomaru Shōzōkan describes their fine detail and bold compositions as features not seen in other painters of the time. ==Roosters, Jakuchū's favourite subject, are at the heart of the series.==

## What it shows

Many kinds of animals and plants, centred on the roosters Jakuchū particularly loved.

## How it was made

Jakuchū was born the eldest son of a greengrocer wholesaler on Nishiki-kōji in Kyoto. According to the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, he handed the family business to his younger brother at 40 and devoted himself to painting. After finishing the 30 scrolls he gave them to Shōkoku-ji together with his own Shaka Triad (Shōkoku-ji collection), and the two were hung together at memorial services. In 1889 (Meiji 22) Shōkoku-ji presented them to the Imperial Household. A letter of donation and a one-line calligraphy by Baisaō, one scroll each, have come down with them.

## What to look at in front of it

- The fine detail of the roosters' feathers, the "minute depiction" the Sannomaru Shōzōkan highlights
- The bold compositions: how objects are placed and how the picture is cropped
- The different subject of each scroll on display

## Where to see it

Museum of the Imperial Collections, Sannomaru Shōzōkan (East Gardens of the Imperial Palace, Chiyoda, Tokyo). The 30 scrolls are not all on view at once; some are shown in each exhibition. Check the museum's website for the schedule.

## FAQ

### How many scrolls are there?

Thirty. A letter of donation and a calligraphy by Baisaō, one scroll each, have also come down with them.

### Where were they originally?

At Shōkoku-ji temple in Kyoto. Jakuchū gave them to the temple, which presented them to the Imperial Household in 1889.

### When did they become a National Treasure?

In 2021 (Reiwa 3).

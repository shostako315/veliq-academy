## Key points

- One sheet of Hokusai's series Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji. In the trough of a great wave, Mount Fuji appears as a small triangle
- A woodblock print of about 1830–32; impressions are held by the Tokyo National Museum, The Metropolitan Museum of Art and others
- One of Hokusai's landscape prints known for indigo and the imported pigment Prussian blue

Under the Wave off Kanagawa is one sheet of Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji, Katsushika Hokusai's series on Mount Fuji. The Metropolitan Museum of Art describes it as an image that represents the art of the world, and notes that it is said to have inspired Debussy's La Mer and Rilke's poem "The Mountain". ==Fuji is small, far in the distance, while the wave in front fills most of the picture.==

## What it shows

A great wave rises offshore. In its trough, far away, Mount Fuji appears as a small triangle through perspective.

## How it was made

A multicolour woodblock print (nishiki-e) in horizontal ōban format. Because it is a woodblock print, several impressions were taken from the same blocks; the Tokyo National Museum, The Metropolitan Museum of Art and others hold them. The Tokyo National Museum's impression is 25.6 cm high and 38.0 cm wide. The Met's impression (H. O. Havemeyer Collection, bequest of 1929) is 25.7 × 37.9 cm, and the Met publishes its image as public domain.

## What to look at in front of it

- Mount Fuji in the trough of the wave, a small triangle
- The difference in size between the wave filling the picture and the distant Fuji
- The blues: indigo and Prussian blue

## Where to see it

The Tokyo National Museum (Ueno, Tokyo) holds an impression. Ukiyo-e prints are sensitive to light and are shown only for short periods; check the museum's website for the schedule. Abroad, The Metropolitan Museum of Art (New York) and others also hold impressions.

## FAQ

### When was it made?

The Metropolitan Museum of Art dates it to about 1830–32. The Tokyo National Museum records it as Edo period, 19th century.

### Is there only one?

No. As a woodblock print, several impressions exist from the same blocks, held by the Tokyo National Museum, The Metropolitan Museum of Art and others.

### What is Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji?

The name of Hokusai's series of prints of Mount Fuji. Under the Wave off Kanagawa is one sheet of it.

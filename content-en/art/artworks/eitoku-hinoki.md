## Key points

- A screen painting of a cypress with a twisting trunk and wide-spreading branches on gold. A National Treasure, held by the Tokyo National Museum
- It is said to have decorated the sliding doors of the residence of Prince Hachijō (later the Katsura-no-miya family), completed in 1590
- Eitoku died in the same year, 1590, aged 48; it is regarded as one of his last works

Cypress Trees is a pair of four-panel folding screens in which Kanō Eitoku painted a giant cypress on gold. It is now a screen, but is said to have been painted originally on sliding doors. ==The trunk twisting across the whole picture and the branches thrusting sideways are its highlight.==

## What it shows

A cypress whose thick trunk rises while twisting, its branches spreading widely to left and right, against a ground of gold leaf. The commentary on Cultural Heritage Online says its bold forms and strong colours express the taste of the period.

## How it was made

It is said to have decorated the sliding doors of the residence of Prince Hachijō, completed in 1590 (Tenshō 18). The Hachijō-no-miya family later became the Katsura-no-miya family. Eitoku was born in Kyoto in 1543; his father was Kanō Shōei and his grandfather Kanō Motonobu. He painted the wall paintings of Azuchi Castle, Osaka Castle and the Jurakudai palace. He died on 14 September 1590, aged 48, and this screen is regarded as one of his last works. The Nihon Daihyakka Zensho considers Eitoku's authorship highly likely.

## What to look at in front of it

- The twist of the trunk, a thick outline running from one edge of the picture to the other
- The contrast between the gold ground and the deep colours of the trunk and leaves
- How the original door composition is divided by the folds, since it was remounted as four-panel screens

## Where to see it

Tokyo National Museum (Ueno, Tokyo). National Treasure screens are shown only for limited periods. Check the museum's website for the schedule.

## FAQ

### When was it painted?

In 1590 (Tenshō 18), the year the Hachijō residence was completed and the year Eitoku died.

### Was it always a screen?

It is said to have been painted as sliding doors for the Hachijō residence. It is now a pair of four-panel screens.

### What are Eitoku's other major works?

They include the Chinese Lions screen at the Museum of the Imperial Collections, Sannomaru Shōzōkan, and the Scenes in and around Kyoto screens that Oda Nobunaga gave to Uesugi Kenshin in 1574.

Yokoyama Taikan — the nihonga painter who founded the Japan Art Institute and created a new ink painting

The life of Yokoyama Taikan (1868–1958) in a timeline: the first class of the Tokyo School of Fine Arts, founding the Japan Art Institute with Okakura Tenshin, the "mōrō-tai" style, the revival of the Institute in 1914, Metempsychosis of 1923, and the first Order of Culture.

Japanese name
横山大観
Dates
1868–1958

Key points

  • Born in Mito on 18 September 1868; his real name was Hidemaro
  • In 1889 he entered the Tokyo School of Fine Arts in its first class, and in 1898 joined Okakura Tenshin and others in founding the Japan Art Institute
  • He revived the Institute in 1914 and received the first Order of Culture in 1937. He died in Tokyo on 26 February 1958

Yokoyama Taikan was a nihonga painter active from the Meiji to the Shōwa period. After founding the Japan Art Institute he experimented with a style called mōrō-tai ("hazy style"), which drew harsh criticism. Metempsychosis of 1923 is regarded as the great work in which his effort to create a new style of ink painting bore fruit.

Style

  • From 1898 he experimented with the style called mōrō-tai
  • He worked on large ink paintings in ink alone

Metempsychosis is a handscroll about 40 m long in ink alone on silk. It shows a drop of water on a mountain becoming a river, reaching the sea, a wave becoming a dragon rising to the sky, and returning to a drop. The National Museum of Modern Art, Tokyo explains that Taikan used many ink techniques and expressed, through the flow of water, how all things change.

Where to see his work

The National Museum of Modern Art, Tokyo (Takebashi) holds Metempsychosis. The whole scroll is unrolled only at certain times; check the museum's website.

FAQ

Where was he born?

In Mito, on 18 September 1868. His real name was Hidemaro.

What is the Japan Art Institute?

An art society founded in 1898 by Okakura Tenshin and others. Taikan took part in its founding and revived it with colleagues in 1914. Its exhibition is the Inten.

When did he receive the Order of Culture?

In 1937, when it was first awarded.

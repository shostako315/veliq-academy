Utagawa Hiroshige — the landscape print artist born into a family of shogunal firemen

The life of Utagawa Hiroshige (1797–1858) in a timeline: heir to a family of fire-brigade officers, apprenticeship with Utagawa Toyohiro, the 1832 journey along the Tōkaidō and the Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō, the Sixty-nine Stations of the Kisokaidō with Keisai Eisen, One Hundred Famous Views of Edo, van Gogh's copies, and where to see his works.

Key points

  • Born in 1797 into the family of an officer of the shogunate's fire brigade at Yayosu-gashi in Edo. His family name was Andō, his childhood name Tokutarō
  • In 1832 he travelled the Tōkaidō to Kyoto with a shogunal procession, and established his name with the Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō (from 1833)
  • His late series One Hundred Famous Views of Edo (from 1856) is also known because van Gogh copied it in oils

Hiroshige depicted the famous sights of Edo and the post towns of the highways with the changing seasons and weather. Many of his sheets put snow, rain, night or an evening shower in their titles; weather is the subject of the picture. He influenced van Gogh and other European painters.

His journey along the Tōkaidō was made with the procession in which the shogunate presented horses to the imperial court. Publication of the Hōeidō Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō began the following year.

Style

  • In the Tenpō era (1830s) he made his name with landscape prints of famous places and highways
  • Many sheets put weather and time of day — rain, snow, night, evening showers — into their titles
  • In his last years he devoted himself to One Hundred Famous Views of Edo, begun in 1856

In the Bunsei era Hiroshige painted beauties, actors and illustrations, but moved to landscape with Famous Places of the Eastern Capital around 1831. His journey up the Tōkaidō to Kyoto in autumn 1832 led to the Hōeidō Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō from the following year.

Titles such as Night Snow at Kambara, Clear Weather after Snow at Nihonbashi, Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi Bridge and Atake, and Night View of Matsuchiyama and Sanya Canal add weather and time of day to the name of the place. The same famous place becomes a different picture on a snowy day and on a day of sudden showers. One Hundred Famous Views of Edo was published from 1856 until the year of his death, and he spent his last few years on this series.

In 1887 van Gogh painted Flowering Plum Orchard, copying Plum Garden at Kameido. He copied the composition exactly and intensified the colours.

One Hundred Famous Views of Edo began in 1856 and was published until the year of Hiroshige's death.

Where to see his work

The Art Institute of Chicago holds, from One Hundred Famous Views of Edo, Chiyogaike Pond at Meguro, Jūmantsubo Plain at Fukagawa Susaki, Fireworks at Ryōgoku and Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi Bridge and Atake, and sheets of the Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō such as Hiratsuka and Fujieda. The Metropolitan Museum of Art holds Night Snow at Kambara, Maple Trees at Mama, Plum Garden at Kamata and Autumn Moon at Ishiyama from Eight Views of Ōmi. Snowy Kambara and the shower over Shin-Ōhashi are worth seeing side by side as Hiroshige's representative pictures of weather. Van Gogh's copy Flowering Plum Orchard is in the Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam.

FAQ

What was Hiroshige's original occupation?

He was born into the family of a fire-brigade officer of the shogunate in Edo. He lost both parents in 1809 and succeeded to the family post at 13.

What are his major series?

The Hōeidō Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō (from 1833) and One Hundred Famous Views of Edo (from 1856).

How did van Gogh learn from Hiroshige?

In 1887 he copied Plum Garden at Kameido in oils, painting Flowering Plum Orchard.

Is the Sixty-nine Stations of the Kisokaidō by Hiroshige alone?

It was made with Keisai Eisen; Hiroshige drew 46 sheets.

What was his real name?

His family name was Andō and his childhood name Tokutarō. His father, Andō Gen'emon, was a fire-brigade officer at Yayosu-gashi in Edo.

Who taught him?

Utagawa Toyokuni's studio had too many applicants, so he entered the studio of Utagawa Toyohiro. He was allowed the name Hiroshige in 1812.

When did he travel the Tōkaidō?

In autumn 1832, with the shogunate's procession presenting horses to the court; the Hōeidō Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō followed from the next year.

When did he die?

In 1858 (Ansei 5, 9th month, 6th day). One Hundred Famous Views of Edo continued to be published until the year of his death.

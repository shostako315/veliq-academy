Sesshū — the Zen monk painter who went to Ming China and gave form to Japanese ink painting

The life of Sesshū Tōyō (generally dated 1420–1506) in a timeline: training at Shōkoku-ji and study under Shūbun, the Unkoku-an studio in Yamaguchi under the patronage of the Ōuchi, the voyage to Ming China in 1467–69, the Long Landscape Scroll, Autumn and Winter Landscapes and View of Amanohashidate, and where to see his works.

Japanese name
雪舟等楊
Dates
1420–1506 (generally given)

Key points

  • Said to have been born in 1420 in Akahama, Bitchū (now Sōja, Okayama). He is thought to have studied painting under Shūbun while training as a Zen monk at Shōkoku-ji in Kyoto
  • In 1467 he sailed to Ming China on an official mission ship and was given the first seat at Tiantong Monastery. He returned in 1469
  • Under the patronage of the Ōuchi family in Yamaguchi he travelled widely, painting real landscapes. He is generally said to have died in 1506

Sesshū was a Zen monk and an ink painter of the Muromachi period. In China he studied many Chinese styles and created a style with firm structure and powerful brushwork. Autumn and Winter Landscapes at the Tokyo National Museum and View of Amanohashidate at the Kyoto National Museum are both National Treasures.

Style

  • He built the depth of landscapes with lines and areas of ink alone
  • He based pictures on real views while adopting the compositions of Chinese landscape painting

Cultural Heritage Online notes that in Autumn and Winter Landscapes the viewer can follow the scenery from front to back and that objects are clearly separated in position — a rational construction not found in earlier landscape painting. View of Amanohashidate is based on a real view, but raises the mountains, stretches the town sideways and takes a viewpoint higher than any real place.

Where to see his work

Autumn and Winter Landscapes is held by the Tokyo National Museum, View of Amanohashidate by the Kyoto National Museum. Both are shown only for limited periods; check each museum's website for the schedule.

FAQ

Where was he born?

Said to be Akahama in Bitchū (now Sōja, Okayama), in 1420.

When did he go to China?

He sailed on a mission ship in 1467 (Ōnin 1) and returned in 1469 (Bunmei 1).

Who taught him?

He is thought to have studied painting under Shūbun at Shōkoku-ji.

When did he die?

Generally given as 1506 (Eishō 3). Encyclopaedias differ on where he died.

Masterpieces in Japanese museums

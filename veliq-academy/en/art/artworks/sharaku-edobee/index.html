Ōtani Oniji III as Yakko Edobei — Tōshūsai Sharaku's bust portrait of an actor

Tōshūsai Sharaku's Ōtani Oniji III as Yakko Edobei (Tokyo National Museum, Important Cultural Property). An ōban colour woodblock print of 1794 (Kansei 6) showing the villain of the kabuki play Koi nyōbō somewake tazuna from the chest up. What it shows, how the hands are drawn, and where it sits in Sharaku's ten-month career.

Tōshūsai Sharaku

Key points

  • A bust portrait of the actor Ōtani Oniji III as Edobei, the villain of the kabuki play Koi nyōbō somewake tazuna
  • The scene in which Edobei attacks the servant Ippei, played by Ichikawa Omezō, to steal the money he carries
  • An ōban bust portrait of 1794 (Kansei 6). Important Cultural Property, held by the Tokyo National Museum

Ōtani Oniji III as Yakko Edobei is a print in which Tōshūsai Sharaku drew an actor large from the chest up. The jutting chin and the eyes glaring upward are said to be designed to pair with a print of his opponent Ippei. The hands have long been pointed out as awkwardly drawn, but this is said to convey murderous intent.

What it shows

Edobei, a character in the kabuki play Koi nyōbō somewake tazuna, attacking the servant Ippei, played by Ichikawa Omezō, to seize the money he carries.

How it was made

A colour woodblock print (nishiki-e) in ōban size (36.9 × 23.7 cm). According to the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho, Sharaku issued some 140 actor prints and other works in about ten months, from the fifth month of 1794 (Kansei 6) to the first month of 1795. His first works, in the fifth month, were 28 ōban bust portraits with dark mica backgrounds (kuro-kira-zuri), published by Tsutaya Jūzaburō. Sharaku's dates of birth and death are unknown.

What to look at in front of it

  • The shape of the two hands, long pointed out as awkwardly drawn
  • The eyes glaring upward and the jutting chin
  • The cropping of the bust portrait (ōkubi-e), showing only the figure from the chest up

Where to see it

Tokyo National Museum (Ueno, Tokyo). Ukiyo-e prints are sensitive to light and are shown only for short periods. Check the museum's website for the schedule.

FAQ

Who is depicted?

The kabuki actor Ōtani Oniji III in the role of Edobei.

How long was Sharaku active?

About ten months, from the fifth month of 1794 to the first month of 1795.

Who published it?

Tsutaya Jūzaburō.

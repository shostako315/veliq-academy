Two Sleeping Children — Rubens's oil sketch on panel, thought to show his late brother's children

Peter Paul Rubens's Two Sleeping Children (c. 1612–13, National Museum of Western Art, Tokyo). An oil study on panel thought to show Clara and Philip, the children of his brother Philip, who died in 1611. Changes revealed by X-ray, and what to look at.

Artist
Peter Paul Rubens
Original title
Two Sleeping Children
Date
c. 1612–13
Medium
Oil on panel
Dimensions
50.5 × 65.5 cm
Collection
National Museum of Western Art, Tokyo

Key points

  • An oil study on panel of the faces of two sleeping infants. Held by the National Museum of Western Art, Tokyo
  • They are thought to be Clara and Philip, the children of Rubens's brother Philip (d. 1611)
  • The same faces were used in Rubens's large paintings, such as the Madonna in a Garland of Flowers in Munich

Two Sleeping Children is a study Rubens painted from life. His close brother Philip died in 1611, and Rubens appears to have painted the children he left, born in 1610 and 1611. From their ages, the work is dated to about 1612–13. X-ray examination has shown that the child on the left was originally awake and looking forward.

What it shows

The faces of two sleeping infants.

How it was made

The National Museum of Western Art says the frank, fresh depiction based on observation, and the handling of the brush — above all the full modelling of the cheeks — show the painter's skill. It is regarded as a clear example of Rubens's realist technique, which renders forms through transparent and opaque colour, contrasts of light and dark, and differences in the thickness of paint.

Rubens was born in Siegen, Germany, in 1577 and grew up in Antwerp. He studied in Italy from 1600 to 1608 and, after returning, became court painter to Archduke Albert and Archduchess Isabella in 1609. This study was made a few years later.

What to look at in front of it

  • The modelling of the cheeks, where the paint is thick and where it is thin
  • The face of the child on the left, originally painted awake and looking forward
  • The size of the panel (50.5 × 65.5 cm)

Where to see it

National Museum of Western Art (Ueno, Tokyo). Works on view in the permanent collection change with rotations. Check the museum's website.

FAQ

Who is depicted?

Thought to be Clara and Philip, the children of Rubens's brother Philip.

When was it painted?

About 1612–13, estimated from the children's ages.

How is it linked to other works?

According to the museum, the two faces were also used in large paintings such as the Madonna in a Garland of Flowers in Munich and a work in the Louvre.

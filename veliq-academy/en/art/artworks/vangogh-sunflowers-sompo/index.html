Sunflowers (SOMPO Museum of Art) — van Gogh's version from the weeks he lived with Gauguin in Arles

Vincent van Gogh's Sunflowers (1888, SOMPO Museum of Art, Tokyo). An oil painting (100.5 × 76.5 cm) thought to have been painted in late November to early December 1888, based on the August Sunflowers in the National Gallery, London. How it entered the collection in 1987, and what to look at.

Key points

  • One of van Gogh's Sunflowers, painted in Arles in 1888. Held by the SOMPO Museum of Art in Shinjuku, Tokyo
  • Based on the Sunflowers with a yellow background of August that year, now in the National Gallery, London
  • It entered the collection in 1987. The museum describes itself as the only museum in Asia where this Sunflowers can be seen

In February 1888 van Gogh moved to Arles in the south of France, planning a shared life with fellow painters. He invited Paul Gauguin, whom he revered as a mentor, and is said to have painted Sunflowers to decorate his room while waiting for him. The SOMPO Museum's version is thought to have been painted in late November to early December 1888, while he was living with Gauguin.

What it shows

Sunflowers. The basic colours and composition are the same as in the National Gallery's Sunflowers.

How it was made

The colour and composition match the London work, but the brushwork and tonality differ. The SOMPO Museum of Art explains that this shows van Gogh did not simply copy, but painted it with renewed thought.

What to look at in front of it

  • The differences in brushwork within the same composition as the London work
  • The differences in tonality: the same scheme of colours, but a different key from London's
  • The size of the canvas, 100.5 cm high

Where to see it

SOMPO Museum of Art (Shinjuku, Tokyo). Check the museum's website for viewing.

FAQ

When was it painted?

In 1888. The SOMPO Museum of Art places it in late November to early December.

Which work is it based on?

The Sunflowers with a yellow background of August 1888, in the National Gallery, London.

When did it come to Japan?

It entered the collection in 1987.

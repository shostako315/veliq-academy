Pine Trees in Snow — Maruyama Ōkyo's snow-covered pines in ink, gold and the white of the paper

Maruyama Ōkyo's Pine Trees in Snow (Mitsui Memorial Museum, National Treasure). A pair of six-panel screens in ink and light colour on paper, painting snow-laden pines without outlines (the mokkotsu technique). The difference between the two screens, the union of sketching from life and decoration, and where to see it.

Artist
Maruyama Ōkyo
Original title
雪松図屏風（Yukimatsu-zu byōbu）
Date
Edo period
Medium
Ink and light colour on paper
Dimensions
Pair of six-panel screens
Collection
Mitsui Memorial Museum
Designation
National Treasure (designated 29 March 1952)

Key points

  • A pair of six-panel screens showing snow-laden pines in ink, gold paint and the white of the paper alone. A National Treasure
  • The pines are painted without outlines, in the mokkotsu ("boneless") technique
  • The right screen shows a straight, powerful trunk; the left a whole pine that bends as it spreads

Pine Trees in Snow is the National Treasure screen that the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho names as Maruyama Ōkyo's masterpiece. It belongs to the Mitsui Memorial Museum. Ōkyo was a mid-Edo painter with ties to the Mitsui family, and the museum lists this screen first among its paintings. The white of the snow is not paint but paper left unpainted.

What it shows

Pines standing in snow, emerging in the light. The right screen has a straight, strong trunk; the left shows a whole pine with a winding trunk.

How it was made

The only materials are ink, gold paint and the white of the paper. The pines are formed with areas of ink rather than outlines, the mokkotsu technique. The Mitsui Memorial Museum regards it as a work that shows well Ōkyo's fresh style, which joined sketching from life with traditional decorative painting.

Ōkyo was born in 1733 to a farming family in Anao village, Tanba Province (now Kameoka, Kyoto Prefecture). He studied under Ishida Yūtei in Kyoto and, while painting megane-e (pictures for optical viewers), encountered Western linear perspective and shading, moving towards a style based on sketching from life.

What to look at in front of it

  • The white of the snow, made by leaving the paper unpainted
  • Where gold paint is used, the only colour besides ink and the white of the paper
  • The different trunks of the two screens, one straight and one bending

Where to see it

Mitsui Memorial Museum (Nihonbashi Muromachi, Tokyo). It is shown only at certain times. Check the museum's website for the schedule.

FAQ

What is it painted with?

Ink, gold paint and the white of the paper only. Its registered name on Cultural Heritage Online is "Shihon tansai yukimatsu-zu".

When was it designated a National Treasure?

On 29 March 1952 (Shōwa 27).

Who was Ōkyo?

A Kyoto painter born in Tanba in 1733 who died in 1795. He valued sketching from life and founded the Maruyama school with his pupils.

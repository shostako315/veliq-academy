Lakeside — Kuroda Seiki's woman in a yukata at Lake Ashi, Hakone

Kuroda Seiki's Lakeside (Tokyo National Museum, Important Cultural Property). An oil painting (69.0 × 84.7 cm) made in summer 1897 on the shore of Lake Ashi in Hakone, with Teruko, later his wife, as the model. Its showings at the Hakuba-kai exhibition and the 1900 Paris Exposition, its pale colours, and where to see it.

Key points

  • An oil painting made over about a month in summer 1897 (Meiji 30) on the shore of Lake Ashi in Hakone
  • The model was Kaneko Taneko, who later became Kuroda's wife, Kuroda Teruko
  • It was shown that autumn at the second Hakuba-kai exhibition under the title "Escaping the Heat", and again at the 1900 Paris Exposition

Lakeside is an oil painting of a woman in a yukata cooling herself with a fan by a lake. A Japanese subject (a woman cooling herself with a fan) is painted in a Western technique, using outdoor light and pale bluish colours while holding back the gloss typical of oil paint. Cultural Heritage Online calls it a representative example of Japanese oil painting. In 1967 it was used as the design of a postage stamp.

What it shows

A woman in a yukata, fan in hand, cooling herself on the shore of Lake Ashi.

How it was made

Kuroda went to France in 1884 to study law, and became a painter after studying under Raphaël Collin. He returned to Japan in 1893 and brought a new style of bright colour. In 1896 he founded the Hakuba-kai with Kume Keiichirō and others and began teaching in the new Western-painting department of the Tokyo School of Fine Arts. Lakeside was painted the following year.

At the 1900 Paris Exposition it was shown as "Au Bord du lac" together with Wisdom, Impression, Sentiment and other works. At the 1927 exhibition of Meiji and Taishō masterpieces it appeared as "Beauty by the Lake". Under Kuroda's will it was donated in 1935 to the Institute of Art Research (now the Tokyo National Research Institute for Cultural Properties) and kept in the Kuroda Memorial Room under the title Lakeside.

What to look at in front of it

  • The pale, bluish colours; the gloss of oil paint is held back
  • The fan in the woman's hand
  • The pale colour of the lake behind her

Where to see it

Held by the Tokyo National Museum and shown at certain times at the Kuroda Memorial Hall in Ueno. Check the websites of the Tokyo National Museum and the Kuroda Memorial Hall for viewing days.

FAQ

Where was it painted?

On the shore of Lake Ashi in Hakone, over about a month in summer 1897.

Who was the model?

Kaneko Taneko, who later became Kuroda's wife, Kuroda Teruko.

What was its first title?

At the second Hakuba-kai exhibition in autumn 1897 it was shown as "Escaping the Heat" (Hisho).

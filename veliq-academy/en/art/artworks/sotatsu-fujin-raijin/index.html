Wind God and Thunder God — Tawaraya Sōtatsu's pair of screens on gold

Tawaraya Sōtatsu's Wind God and Thunder God (Kennin-ji temple, on deposit at the Kyoto National Museum, National Treasure). A pair of two-panel screens, each 154.5 × 169.8 cm, with the wind god and thunder god on a gold ground. Why it is attributed to Sōtatsu's late years despite having no signature, its link to Ogata Kōrin and Sakai Hōitsu, and where to see it.

Tawaraya Sōtatsu

Date
Edo period
Medium

Key points

  • The wind god and the thunder god on a pair of two-panel screens with a gold ground. Owned by Kennin-ji temple in Kyoto and designated a National Treasure
  • It has no signature or seal, but is regarded as a genuine work and the masterpiece of Sōtatsu's late years
  • The original is on deposit at the Kyoto National Museum; Kennin-ji displays a high-definition replica

Wind God and Thunder God shows the god of wind and the god of thunder on two separate screens. About a hundred years later Ogata Kōrin copied it by laying thin paper over it, and Sakai Hōitsu then painted Summer and Autumn Grasses on the back of Kōrin's screens. Through this one subject one can follow the Rinpa line from Sōtatsu to Kōrin and Hōitsu.

What it shows

The wind god is on one two-panel screen, the thunder god on the other. The ground is gold leaf; each screen is 154.5 cm high and 169.8 cm wide. The Tokyo National Museum explains that in Sōtatsu's screens the thunder god looks down on the world below, whereas in Kōrin's copy he looks at the wind god.

How it was made

Colour on paper laid with gold leaf. Kennin-ji states that although the work has no signature or seal, it is regarded as genuine and as the finest work of Sōtatsu's late years. Sōtatsu's dates are unknown. He appears to have run a painting shop called Tawaraya in Kyoto, and painted gold and silver underpaintings for the calligraphy of Hon'ami Kōetsu. By 1630 (Kan'ei 7) he held the honorary rank of hokkyō. He is credited with creating tarashikomi, dropping another colour into wet ink or pigment so that it spreads.

What to look at in front of it

  • The thunder god's gaze, looking down on the world below (in Kōrin's copy he looks at the wind god)
  • The wide gold ground between the two gods
  • Compared with Kōrin's copy, the position of the gods and the size of the clouds; the Kyoto National Museum notes that Kōrin's clouds are darker and larger

Where to see it

The original is on deposit at the Kyoto National Museum and is sometimes shown in its exhibitions. Kennin-ji (Higashiyama, Kyoto) displays high-definition replica screens.

FAQ

Who owns it?

Kennin-ji temple in Kyoto. It is now on deposit at the Kyoto National Museum.

Is it signed by Sōtatsu?

No. It has no signature or seal, but is regarded as his genuine work.

How is it related to Kōrin's Wind God and Thunder God?

Ogata Kōrin copied Sōtatsu's picture by laying thin paper over it. Kōrin's screens (Important Cultural Property) are in the Tokyo National Museum. Sakai Hōitsu painted Summer and Autumn Grasses on their back; the front and back were later separated for conservation.
